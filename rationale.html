Why bother with an Origin CA cert?

No jargon. If the technical walkthrough didn't click, read this one first — it explains the whole idea using an office building, a reception desk, and an ID badge.

🏢 1. Think of your website like an office building

Cloudflare is the reception desk at the front of the building — every visitor talks to reception first. Your origin server (the real app, on Fly.io, a VPS, wherever) is the back office, behind a door reception can reach but the public can't.

🔒 2. Lock #1: Visitor ↔ Reception — already solved

When someone visits your site, their browser and Cloudflare's reception desk talk over a locked, sealed line — that's the padlock 🔒 in the browser's address bar. Cloudflare provides and manages this automatically.

✅ You don't do anything for this one — it's already handled.

🚪 3. Lock #2: Reception ↔ Back Office — this is the gap

Here's the part people miss: reception still has to walk down a hallway to reach the back office. If that hallway is unlocked:

  • 🕵️ Anyone with access to the internal hallway could eavesdrop on what reception tells the back office.
  • 🎭 Someone could impersonate the back office, and reception would have no way to tell.

That hallway is the connection between Cloudflare's edge and your origin server. Nothing locks it by default.

🪪 4. What the Origin CA certificate actually is

It's a special ID badge that only Cloudflare issues, and only Cloudflare trusts. You pin this badge on your back-office door (your origin server). Now:

  • 🧐 Reception checks the badge every time before handing over any visitor's data.
  • 🚫 Nobody else can forge it — only Cloudflare's own badge-printing machine made it.
  • ⏳ The public never needs to see or trust this badge, which is why it can stay valid for up to 15 years instead of the ~90 days a public certificate lasts.

🙅 5. "Why not just make my own lock?"

You could put any lock on the back-office door (a self-signed certificate). But if it's a lock you made yourself, reception has no way to know it's genuinely yours and not an impostor's. Cloudflare's strictest mode, Full (strict), refuses to trust a lock it didn't issue or recognize — which is the entire point: it stops a fake back office from ever being trusted.

😬 7. What happens if you skip this

Without an Origin CA cert, you're forced into Cloudflare's "Flexible" SSL mode: the front door is locked, but the internal hallway isn't. Visitor traffic looks secure in the browser, but it travels in the clear between Cloudflare and your server.

⚠️ Anyone who can reach that hallway (a misconfigured network, a compromised host nearby, certain interception setups) can read or alter the traffic. The Origin CA cert closes that gap for about ten minutes of setup.
